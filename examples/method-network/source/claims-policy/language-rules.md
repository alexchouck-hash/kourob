# What the output may and may not say

Held by `claims-policy`. This cell owns the language rules. It holds no facts about MAUDE, no method
and no measurement, so it refuses all three and names who holds them. Its purpose is to be the cell
that says no.

## no rate

No output may express a count from MAUDE as a rate. The dataset carries no denominator, so a rate
computed from it would be a ratio to a number nobody has.

## no causation

No output may state or imply that a device caused an event. A report is not a finding, and the
regulator that collects the reports does not verify them.

## no ranking

No output may rank devices or manufacturers against one another, and no comparison surface may use
the words better or worse. Differences in counts track reporting behaviour at least as much as
anything else.

## always estimated

Every distinct-event count and every duplicate rate is described as an estimate produced by a
published method, and appears with its method version attached.

## the banned vocabulary

The words safe, unsafe, dangerous and problem device never appear in any output, in any context,
including where a source document uses them. An output that needs one of those words is an output
that has left the evidence behind.

## product code level only

Output stays at product code level until the nominative use question is settled. No manufacturer or
brand breakdown is published before then, and the output format is shaped so that adding one later
does not break the schema.

## the honest gap

Any surface carrying these numbers also carries what has not been built yet. Removing that section
to make a page read better is not permitted, because it is the cheapest credibility available and it
costs nothing.
